The Origin and Purpose of the European Day of Languages

The European Day of Languages originated in 2001 during the European Year of Languages, organized by the Council of Europe and the European Union to celebrate linguistic diversity across the continent. Due to the tremendous success of this initiative, which involved millions of people across 45 countries, the Council of Europe officially declared September 26 as the European Day of Languages to be celebrated every year.

This day was established to raise awareness about the immense importance of language learning. Knowing multiple languages enhances intercultural understanding and creates new educational and professional opportunities. It also promotes the preservation of Europe’s rich linguistic and cultural heritage while encouraging people of all ages to learn new languages throughout their lives.

How many languages?
Europe has over 225 native languages.
Albanian is special!
Albanian is an independent branch of the Indo-European language tree. It has no direct “sister” languages, making it uniquely historic.

✨ Unique Words (No Direct Translation)

These fascinating European words describe feelings, habits, or concepts that take an entire sentence to explain in English or other languages.

Sobremesa
Spanish
The period of time spent relaxing around the table after a meal, engaging in conversation long after the food is finished.
Schadenfreude
German
The secret feeling of pleasure or satisfaction derived from witnessing someone else’s minor misfortune.
Abbiocco
Italian
The heavy, sleepy drowsiness felt right after eating a large, satisfying meal—a food coma.
Flâner
French
The art of wandering aimlessly through city streets with no destination or purpose other than experiencing the atmosphere.
Hygge
Danish
A cozy, warm, and comfortable atmosphere that brings a feeling of contentment and well-being.
Lagom
Swedish
Not too little, not too much; just the right amount, perfectly balanced.
Saudade
Portuguese
A deep, melancholic longing or nostalgia for a person, place, or time that is far away or gone forever.
Meraki
Greek
Putting your soul, passion, creativity, and love into whatever task you are doing.
Besa
Albanian
A sacred pledge, word of honor, or solemn promise that guarantees absolute loyalty and protection under any circumstances.
Tartle
Scottish / English dialect
The brief, awkward hesitation right before introducing someone because you have momentarily forgotten their name.
